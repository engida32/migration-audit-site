The audit ends with a decision, not a deck.

Every finding is ranked by consequence and effort. You leave with one of four calls, and the first move for each one.

Migration decision table
DecisionWhen it fitsWhat you leave with
FixThe system can stay. The risk is contained and the patch path is short.A short remediation sequence and the tests that prove it held.
ExtendThe current platform is worth another cycle, but one area needs rework first.A bounded extension plan and the risks that would make it fail.
ReplaceThe cost of carrying the system is higher than a controlled move.A cutover sequence, rollback gate, the first three work packages, and the decision gates that follow.
LeaveThe risk is acceptable for now, or the timing is wrong.A written case for waiting, with the triggers that change the answer.

What you get in five days.

  • One-page findings summary. The decision, the top risks, the cost of waiting, and a scope statement that says what was examined and what was not.
  • A ranked risk register. Severity, consequence, and effort for each finding, not a flat list.
  • A phase-1 implementation plan. A three-phase sequence: stop the bleeding, stabilize, then migrate. Phase 1 is detailed to the first three moves, with dependencies, effort, acceptance criteria, and a rollback gate. Phases 2 and 3 are mapped at decision-gate level.
  • A 30-minute readout. The decision in plain language, with room for the questions your team will ask.
  • An editable risk register. So your team can keep using it after the audit ends.

Five days, one sequence.

The window is fixed so the decision does not drift. Each day has one job and one output.

  1. Day 1

    Intake and scope

    We confirm the system, the migration path, and the decision you need to make.

  2. Day 2

    Architecture and dependencies

    We map the parts that move together, the parts that cannot, and the version gaps.

  3. Day 3

    Data, auth, and integrations

    We trace the identifiers, permissions, and external calls that break in a cutover.

  4. Day 4

    Migration path and rollback

    We test the sequence on paper, name the failure points, and set the rollback gate.

  5. Day 5

    Findings and readout

    You get the decision, the ranked register, the phase-1 implementation plan, and a 30-minute readout.

Questions before you book.

If your question is not here, bring it to the 30-minute call. The call is for scoping, not a sales pitch.

What counts as one system?

One application and the services it directly depends on. If the migration also changes a second product or a shared platform, we will scope that before the work starts.

Do you need production data?

No. A repository, a read-only copy, or a sanitized export is enough. If a finding needs a data shape, we can work from a schema and a small synthetic sample.

Can you sign an NDA?

Yes. We can use your NDA or a mutual one. Access is named, read-only where possible, and working files are deleted after 14 days unless you ask us to keep them.

What if the repository is a mess?

That is normal for legacy systems. The audit starts with the parts that carry the migration risk, not with a cleanup project. If the mess blocks the decision, that becomes the first finding.

What happens after the audit?

You get the decision, the register, and the phase-1 implementation plan. You can run the work with your team, bring in another partner, or ask us to quote the first implementation package. Implementation is a separate engagement, quoted after the readout so the scope and price match the work. The audit fee credits toward the first implementation package if you proceed within 30 days of the readout, and the credit is confirmed in the implementation agreement. The audit does not lock you into implementation.

Can you implement the migration?

Yes, as a separate engagement. The audit is read-only and fixed-fee. Implementation needs write access, a schedule, and its own scope. We quote the first package after the readout, with acceptance criteria and a rollback plan. The audit fee credits toward the first implementation package if you proceed within 30 days of the readout, and the credit is confirmed in the implementation agreement. You can also take the phase-1 plan to your team or another vendor.

Why is the plan phase 1, not the full migration?

A full implementation plan needs production behavior, team capacity, and vendor constraints that sit outside a five-day read-only audit. The audit details the first three moves and maps phases 2 and 3 at decision-gate level. That is enough to start, price, and sequence the work without pretending the whole migration is known on day one.

Why is the fee fixed?

Because the scope is fixed: one system, one repository, one migration path, five business days. If the work needs a wider review, we will tell you before it starts rather than surprise you with a change order.

Can we do this remotely?

Yes. The audit runs on a repository, a read-only copy, or a sanitized export. The readout is a 30-minute call. No travel is required.

Who will I speak with?

The auditor who writes the report. There is no sales handoff. The call is for scoping, not a pitch.
